**tongue in cheek** (/ˌtʌŋ ɪn ˈtʃiːk/) is an idiom describing something said as a joke while appearing to be serious. Before a noun, it is normally hyphenated: **a tongue-in-cheek remark**.

Source: [Cambridge — tongue in cheek](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/tongue-in-cheek).

## Examples

Cambridge describes praise that may actually be a joke:

> He said that he was a huge fan of the president, although I suspect it was tongue in cheek.

— [Cambridge — tongue in cheek](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/tongue-in-cheek).

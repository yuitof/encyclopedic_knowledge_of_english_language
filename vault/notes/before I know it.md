before I know it
: 

- Before I knew it, I'd gotten accustomed to this drifter's life… It's a strange thing. In the past I never imagined this was how I'd end up.

**before I knew it** (/bɪˈfɔːr aɪ ˈnjuː ɪt/) is the past-tense form of **before I know it**, an idiom meaning that something happens very quickly, before the speaker has time to realize it. The dictionary form is often **before you know it**. ([Cambridge Dictionary](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/before-you-know-it))

**Know → knew** changes the expression to past tense; **before** and **it** stay the same.

## Examples

Crispy and his companion are trying to catch their flight to Easter Island, but it is too late to check their bags. An airport employee suggests bag storage. Before Crispy has fully understood this option, they are already running through the airport again. The phrase emphasizes how quickly events unfold.

> We had no idea what bag storage was, but before I knew it we started running again.

— [I went to Easter Island — Crispy Concords, 14:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DEFiZIcjbyY&t=899s)

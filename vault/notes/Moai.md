**moai** (/ˈmoʊ.aɪ/) is a large stone statue carved by the Rapa Nui people of Easter Island (Rapa Nui). These figures represent ancestral chiefs. ([The Metropolitan Museum of Art](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/easter-island); [Wiktionary: pronunciation and word forms](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/moai))

Countable noun; plural **moai** or **moais**. The figures have prominent heads and elongated faces, with torsos and arms as well.

## Examples

Crispy introduces Rano Raraku, the site where many of Easter Island's moai were carved from stone. Here, *moai quarry* means the place that supplied stone for making the statues. ([Chile Travel](https://chile.travel/en/itineraries/heritage-route-te-ara-o-te-moai-circuit/))

> It's a place called Rano Raku which in English just translates to the moai quarry.[^quarry]

— [I went to Easter Island — Crispy Concords, 38:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DEFiZIcjbyY&t=2323s)

[^quarry]: **quarry** (/ˈkwɒri/) is a countable noun meaning a place where stone is cut or dug out of the ground. Here it refers to the stone-working site where the moai were made. ([Oxford Advanced Learner’s Dictionary](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/us/definition/english/quarry_1))

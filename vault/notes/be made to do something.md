**be made to do something** (/bi meɪd tə duː ˈsʌmθɪŋ/) is the passive construction of causative **make**, meaning to be forced or caused to do something.

The active pattern is **make + object + bare [[infinitive]]**. The passive pattern restores **to**: **be made + to-[[infinitive]]**. Causative **make** can also express a result, such as causing laughter, without literal compulsion.

## Examples

Cambridge illustrates the passive pattern.

> The people were made to wait outside while the committee reached its decision.

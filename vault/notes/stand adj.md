**stand** (/stænd/) can be a linking verb in the pattern **stand + adjective**. The adjective describes the state the subject is in. For example, **stand ready** means to be prepared to act. [Oxford Advanced Learner’s Dictionary, “stand”](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/definition/english/stand_1)

**Stand clear of** means to keep away from something or stay out of its way. Oxford and Cambridge label **clear** as an adverb in this expression. [Oxford Advanced Learner’s Dictionary, “clear”](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/definition/english/clear_3) · [Cambridge Dictionary, “clear”](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/english/clear)

## Examples

The New York City subway announcement asks passengers to move away from the doors so they can close.

> Stand clear of the closing doors, please.

[NYC Sounds, NYC Tourism + Conventions](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zQh1dTgsK8U&t=0s)

New York City Council members use **stand ready** to say they are prepared to help.

> As members of the New York City Council, we stand ready to help protect academic freedom and freedom of speech.

[New York City Council open letter](https://council.nyc.gov/shahana-hanif/wp-content/uploads/sites/111/2025/04/progressive-caucus-letter-to-colleges.pdf)

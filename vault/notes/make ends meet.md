**make ends meet** (/ˌmeɪk endz ˈmiːt/) is an idiom meaning to have just enough money to pay for the things one needs.

## Examples

Discussing why someone deserves help, the speaker says:

> "She's managing a job and she's doing eel after the job to make ends meet."

[The $1 vs $1000 Epal Experience!](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QUTTpy9FQd4&t=1147) — percgripper223, 19:07; watched 25 October and 7 November 2026.

The automatic caption reads **eel** after **doing**; that word was not audio-verified.

In a short English lesson, the speaker gives a clearer explanation:

> "In English, when you try to make ends meet, it means that you try to earn enough money at your job so that you can pay all of your expenses."

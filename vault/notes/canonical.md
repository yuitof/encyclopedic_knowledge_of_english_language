## Mathematics

**canonical** (/kəˈnɑːnɪkəl/) is an adjective describing a standard form or a [[natural]] construction in mathematics. For constructions, it often means determined by the given structure without arbitrary choices. The precise meaning depends on the context.

- **Canonical form:** a standard representation. A rational number can be written uniquely as a reduced fraction with coprime numerator and denominator and a positive denominator; for example, $6/8$ becomes $3/4$.
- **Canonical map:** a map naturally determined by the structure. For a vector space $V$ and a subspace $W$, the quotient map $v \mapsto v+W$ sends each vector to its coset in $V/W$, without choosing a basis.

Sources: [Merriam-Webster](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/canonical), [Wolfram MathWorld: Canonical](https://mathworld.wolfram.com/Canonical.html), [Wolfram MathWorld: Canonical Map](https://mathworld.wolfram.com/CanonicalMap.html).

Adverb: [[canonically]].

**you know what I mean?** (/juː noʊ wʌt aɪ miːn/) is an informal way to check that the listener understands the point you just made. It often invites a quick acknowledgment. **You know what I'm saying?** (/juː noʊ wʌt aɪm ˈseɪɪŋ/) serves the same purpose in casual speech. [Cambridge Dictionary](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/english/you-know-what-i-mean) · [Cambridge English Grammar Today](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/grammar/british-grammar/chunks)

## Examples

The speaker is running out of things to do in VR and checks that the other person understands. The other person answers **yeah**.

> I'm running out of, like, a reason to do stuff. You know what I mean?

[I Spent 7 Days In VR, Here's What It Does To Your Brain, 35:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=efHFzNrDG5Y&t=2116s)

A player asks Keith for a place where the whole group can gather. He adds **you know what I'm saying?** to check that Keith understands the request.

> Yo, Keith, do you have a place where we could all, like, go to where it's a bunch of us? You know what I'm saying?

[VR Scavenger Hunt, 2:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fEkfv2nwl0A&t=177s)

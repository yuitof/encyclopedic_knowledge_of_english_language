**anyway** (/ˈɛniˌweɪ/) is an adverb that signals you're changing the subject or returning to an earlier one. It often comes at the start of the next sentence and can sound neutral. [Oxford Advanced American Dictionary](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/us/definition/american_english/anyway)

**whatever** (/wətˈevər/) is an informal response that signals you've stopped caring about what's being discussed or want to drop it. You can say it on its own before moving on. It can sound dismissive, especially when it's directed at someone else's point. [Oxford Advanced Learner’s Dictionary](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/us/definition/english/whatever_1)

## Examples

After the players discuss whether something counts as a point, one uses **anyway** to move on to the next item on the list.

> Anyway, what's next on the list?

> It feels better, you know? Whatever. Moving on.
